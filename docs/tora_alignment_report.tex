\begin{abstract}
We are connecting the obgx research stack (place/trade $\rightarrow$ book
reconstruction $\rightarrow$ \texttt{tick\_table} $\rightarrow$ cqr bar
graphs $\rightarrow$ cc models) to a live Chinese-equity feed --- the TORA
(HuaXin Qidian) native L2 API, which delivers order-by-order order and trade
streams plus a 10-level book snapshot stream. The central question is the
alignment between \emph{our data} (the reconstructed research tick tape) and
\emph{their live data} (the vendor L2 streams). We extracted the exact
row-generation contract from the Rust engine (cn\_rst3), re-dressed the
research tape as a TORA-shaped capture, and measured the live pipeline's
fidelity on real 20220104 data: \textbf{98.4\% cell-exact} across the
statelessly-representable rows (21{,}719 rows), with event codes, signs and
volumes each \textbf{99.9\%} exact. The residual deltas are precisely
attributed, and the strategy to close them --- reconstruct the book from the
vendor's own order-by-order updates and use the vendor's level snapshots only
as a \emph{reference} for the reconstruction --- is specified here together
with the remaining per-order row-mutation port.
\end{abstract}

\section{The two data worlds}

Research-side (our data):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{place/ + trade/} raw parquet (the exchange feed in broker
        format: order adds/cancels, trades), consumed by cn\_rst3;
  \item \textbf{book reconstruction} --- the Rust engine maintains a full
        limit-order book with FIFO slots from place/trade and emits the
        \textbf{tick\_table}: 83 columns (24 core + 60 book), one row per
        executable event, prices in integer cents, timestamps in ns past
        midnight;
  \item tick\_table feeds the cqr bar graphs (per 3m window) and onward to
        the cc models. Bar fidelity to this tape is pinned bit-faithfully.
\end{itemize}

Live-side (their data): the TORA L2 API (\texttt{TORALEV2API} v4.0.8)
delivers three streams per instrument:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{md} --- 10-level book snapshot (\texttt{Lev2MarketDataField}):
        prices (yuan), quantities, per-level order counts, session state
        (auction/continuous), cumulative volume;
  \item \textbf{od} --- order-by-order order stream
        (\texttt{Lev2OrderDetailField}): add/delete of resting orders with
        order number, price, quantity, side, order status;
  \item \textbf{tr} --- order-by-order trade stream
        (\texttt{Lev2TransactionField}): price, quantity, buy/sell order
        numbers, aggressor flag (BSFlag), sequence numbers; resend variants
        exist for gap recovery.
\end{itemize}

\section{Row-generation contract}
\label{sec:contract}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Code & Meaning & \texttt{vol\_placed} & \texttt{vol\_traded} \\
\midrule
1 & Add (limit order to book) & $+q$ & 0 \\
2 & Cancel & $-q$ & 0 \\
4 & Auction add / auction fill & $+q$ / $0$ & $0$ / $q$ \\
5 & Market / aggressive-intent row & $+q$ & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two structural facts drive the live design:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Trades do not emit rows.} A continuous fill \emph{mutates}
        the aggressor's existing Add row (\texttt{vol\_traded}, \texttt{tvr},
        \texttt{count\_traded}, \texttt{depth\_traded}) and the updated row is
        \emph{re-emitted} (sweep-confirm re-emission). A stateless mapper
        cannot reproduce this; it requires the per-order row-mutation port.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{level\_placed}} is the number of distinct price
        levels between the best price and the order's price (0 at best,
        $-1$ crossed, $-2$ auction fills) computed on the \emph{full} book
        --- values $\geq 10$ appear in the tape. A 10-level feed alone can
        only compute it while the order price is within the visible levels
        (the cqr graph's \texttt{\_lle1} variants need only the top two
        levels and remain exact).
\end{enumerate}

Book snapshots on a row are \emph{pre-event} (the book as it stood before the
row's own event). Auction windows (empirical, sh600000/20220104): adds
09:15--09:25 and 14:57--15:00; the auction fill print is a single instant
(09:25:00.980 open --- 67 fan-out rows at one price; 15:00:00.000 close).

\section{Methodology: the re-dress dry run}
\label{sec:method}

Because the TORA wire and the research tape describe the same underlying
market, the live pipeline can be validated \emph{offline} on past data
aligned with place/trade:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Re-dress}: research place $\rightarrow$ TORA \texttt{od}
        lines; trade $\rightarrow$ \texttt{tr} lines; tick\_table book
        columns $\rightarrow$ \texttt{md} lines --- all in the exact wire
        format of the C++ capture tool (\texttt{lev2\_dump.cpp}).
  \item \textbf{Normalize} (\texttt{normalize.py}): merge the three streams
        in time order, maintain the last 10-level book, stamp every event
        pre-event, dedupe resends in the live sequence space, map codes
        per \S\ref{sec:contract}.
  \item \textbf{Convert} (\texttt{converters.py}): events $\rightarrow$ the
        canonical 83-column tick\_table schema.
  \item \textbf{Profile} (\texttt{profiler.py}): cellwise comparison against
        the research tick\_table keyed on
        (\texttt{exch\_ts}\,ms, price, qty), with per-column mismatch
        attribution, NaN-exact semantics, and missing-row/column reporting.
\end{enumerate}

The loop is pinned by \texttt{tests/test\_synth\_tora.py}; the identical
chain grades any real capture from the live front.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Baseline: the capture$\rightarrow$event$\rightarrow$convert round-trip on the
research tape itself is \textbf{bit-faithful} --- sh600000/20220104,
30{,}598 rows $\times$ 83 columns, \textbf{100.0\%} exact
(\texttt{tests/test\_alignment.py}).

Dry run (real 20220104, adds/cancels/auction fills --- the statelessly
representable 73\% of the tape):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Metric & Value \\
\midrule
Rows compared & 21{,}719 \\
Overall cell-exact & \textbf{98.4\%} \\
Event-code mismatches & 25 \quad (99.9\%) \\
Sign mismatches & 15 \quad (99.9\%) \\
\texttt{vol\_placed}/\texttt{vol\_traded} mismatches & 135 / 122 \quad (99.9\%) \\
\texttt{level\_placed} mismatches & 5{,}373 \\
Book qty/order-count mismatches & $\sim$1{,}100 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Residual attribution:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{level\_placed} beyond the visible 10 levels}
        (5{,}373 rows): a structural limit of any 10-level feed; closed by
        the reconstruction strategy of \S\ref{sec:strategy}.
  \item \textbf{Book-stamp timing at fan-out instants} ($\sim$1{,}100):
        at multi-fill instants the pre-event book differs by the fill
        sequence; closed by reconstructing the book from the tick stream
        itself rather than from snapshots.
  \item \textbf{Continuous trades} (7{,}749 rows): mutation-only rows;
        require the per-order row-mutation port (\S\ref{sec:port}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Level and book strategy: reconstruct from ticks, use their levels
as reference}
\label{sec:strategy}

The vendor sends both the order-by-order event stream \emph{and} its own
book snapshots. The proposed strategy is to invert their roles relative to
a snapshot-trusting design:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Primary book = our reconstruction from their ticks.} Port
        the cn\_rst3 book machinery (limit-order book with FIFO slots, cages,
        sweeps) driven by the TORA \texttt{od}/\texttt{tr} primitives --- the
        same add/cancel/fill events the research reconstruction consumes.
        This yields the full-depth book, exact \texttt{level\_placed}, exact
        pre-event stamping, and the mutation/re-emission semantics
        (\S\ref{sec:port}) --- i.e., the whole 83-column tape.
  \item \textbf{Vendor levels = reference, not source.} Their 10-level
        snapshots validate the reconstruction: per-level cross-checks at
        snapshot instants, divergence alerts, and drift statistics. The
        vendor book is never used as the primary book state.
  \item \textbf{Why this order.} (a) The research pipeline itself
        reconstructs --- parity requires reconstructing; (b) the vendor
        snapshot timing relative to their own events is unknown until M0
        captures (the dry run's book-stamp deltas are exactly this
        uncertainty); (c) beyond-10 information only exists in the
        reconstruction.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The remaining port}
\label{sec:port}

The per-order row-mutation port is the one substantive implementation left:
an OrderBook with FIFO slots and cages, per-order row state mutated by fills,
sweep handling (SZ), and re-emission --- verified offline against
place/trade $\rightarrow$ tick\_table at 100.0\% (the same gate
\texttt{scripts/gate\_stream\_vs\_batch.py} applies to the Rust engine).
The TORA feed supplies exactly the primitives the port consumes, so the live
engine then reproduces the full research semantics on a live feed.

\section{Live capture status}
\label{sec:live}

The official SDK (\texttt{API\_Stock\_lv2\_C++ v4.0.8}) is in hand; the C++
capture tool is built and linked; both fronts (7$\times$24 and simulation)
are reachable from the working host. First login at 06:15 Beijing returned
no response (pre-market and/or the account-login DepartmentID requirement);
a capture is scheduled for the 09:15 Beijing market open. Every capture is
graded by the exact machinery of \S\ref{sec:method}.

\section{Conclusion}

The alignment between our research tape and the TORA live feed is now a
measured, attributed quantity rather than an assumption: 98.4\% cell-exact
with 99.9\% on the semantic core, and a precise closing plan --- tick-driven
book reconstruction validated against vendor levels, plus the per-order
mutation port --- that targets full parity on the live feed.
